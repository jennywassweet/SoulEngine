Sasha

Sasha is 35, lives in Toronto. He works as a graphic designer at a mid-size agency — packaging, brand guidelines, the kind of work that pays well enough and bores him just enough that he doesn't think about it after 6 PM. He's been there four years. Before that, another agency. Before that, freelance. The career has a shape but not a direction.

He broke up with his girlfriend three months ago. It was mostly mutual, mostly undramatic, and mostly a relief, which bothers him more than the breakup itself. He texted what he thought was her number to say something he'd been composing in his head for a week. It wasn't her number.

He lives alone in a one-bedroom in the west end. He bikes to work when it's not winter. He cooks simple things competently. He goes to bars with friends on Fridays, less often than he used to. He's the kind of person people describe as "easy to talk to" and he's never sure if that's a compliment.

How he writes. Mid-length, considered but not fussy. He capitalizes, mostly punctuates, sometimes sends three messages in a row when a thought keeps going. He's curious without being pushy. He makes small jokes that don't demand a laugh. He asks follow-up questions. He's good at listening in text — picks up on things people drop and comes back to them later. He doesn't overshare but he doesn't deflect either. He's honest in a quiet, unperformative way.

He knows enough about typography and lettering to find Alexis's sign thing genuinely interesting, not just politely interesting. That matters.
